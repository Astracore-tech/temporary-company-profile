# AstraCore Technologies / Amplifyr operating plan

Status: proposed operating model implemented in the website. Founded in 2023. This is a practical launch plan, not a forecast or evidence of existing revenue. Commercial package scopes are proposed; capacity, costs, and fulfilment must be confirmed before accepting a kickoff.

## 1. Positioning and offer hierarchy

AstraCore Technologies is the Kenya-based company. Amplifyr is its flagship creator-marketing product, not a separate legal entity implied by the website.

Promise: build the digital foundation, then help the brand tell a useful story through relevant creators. The website is a natural starting point, not a mandatory purchase before Amplifyr.

Primary initial customer: an owner-led service, retail, hospitality, or product business with a clear offer, reachable decision-maker, capacity to fulfil demand, and an approved content budget. Begin with Kenya; treat US/global expansion as a separate delivery cohort with USD proposals and explicitly agreed time-zone coverage.

Do not sell every technical capability in the first conversation. Identify one urgent problem: a weak website, missed enquiries, manual operations, or an unstructured content programme. Choose the smallest useful engagement.

## 3. Thirty-day trial: a service-fee waiver, not unlimited free production

The public offer is KES 0 or USD 0 for AstraCore campaign-management fees during the first 30 days. Website work is paid separately. Creator labour, ad spend, production, travel, and software are not promised for free.

Suggested initial capacity: at most three simultaneous trial businesses, with an eight-hour internal coordination budget per trial. These are internal starting assumptions, not public capacity claims. Adjust after measuring actual delivery time.

Eligibility: one trial per business; clear product/offer; authorised decision-maker; usable website or enquiry destination; prompt approvals; realistic third-party budget or separately agreed content arrangement. Record business identity and prior trial history during qualification. An application is not an automatic acceptance.

Included: discovery; one campaign brief; up to three creator candidates subject to fit and availability; coordination for one chosen creator and one short-form concept; one consolidated feedback round; closing review. Deliverable quantity, posting, paid usage, and creator compensation must be stated in the approved creator scope.

Day zero: confirm brief, costs, rights, dates, success measure, and approvals in writing. Start the trial clock only after agreement. If there is no viable creator or budget, revise the plan before starting.

Days 1-7: brief and baseline. Days 8-14: shortlist and agreements. Days 15-21: content coordination and review. Days 22-30: delivery review, available measurement, and optional retainer proposal. Content production depends on creator availability and client approvals; write down any revised dates.

No automatic paid renewal. No guarantee of sales, reach, or virality. A client can stop future coordination; previously authorised external commitments follow their agreed cancellation terms.

## 4. Pricing and contribution economics

Do not publish invented package prices. Establish sustainable prices from measured costs before quoting. Proposals use KES for the Kenya/Africa experience and USD for global work; do not imply one is a live exchange conversion of the other.

Internal price floor = (direct delivery cost + allocated tools + risk reserve) / (1 - target contribution margin).

Illustrative planning example only: KES 18,000 direct delivery + KES 2,000 tools + KES 4,000 reserve = KES 24,000. At a proposed 40% contribution margin, the service-fee floor would be KES 40,000. This is not a published price or forecast. Taxes and creator compensation are outside this example and need proper treatment in actual quotes.

Charge approved creator and advertising budgets separately. Do not finance client ad spend or creator fees from the trial waiver. Agree funding, cancellation, and reconciliation before any booking. Use written milestone acceptance and change requests for website/system projects.

Before approving a trial, calculate expected acquisition cost: coordination hours x loaded internal cost, plus any expressly approved subsidy. Compare this with likely contribution from a paid retainer; do not assume a conversion rate. Stop adding trials when delivery capacity or cash reserves cannot support them.

## 5. Customer journey and ownership

Website check -> optional enquiry -> qualification -> scoped proposal or trial fit check -> accepted scope -> kickoff -> delivery/approval -> review -> optional monthly plan.

Lead desk statuses implemented: new, qualified, proposal, trial, active, closed. An owner should review new enquiries at least each business day. The website promises no response SLA until actual staffing is confirmed.

Qualification fields: business/offer, target customer, country, website, current bottleneck, intended outcome, budget range, decision-maker, timeline, third-party requirements. Do not collect sensitive client credentials in the enquiry form.

Handoff: the lead owner records the agreed scope; the delivery owner confirms access and acceptance criteria; the campaign owner confirms creator terms; the finance owner confirms approved budgets. Initially one person can hold multiple roles, but each task still needs a named owner.

Closing checklist: written scope accepted, project files handed over, credentials transferred securely, outstanding issues recorded, support period defined, and final payment/reconciliation completed.

## 6. Creator supply and campaign operations

Build a creator roster before promising availability. Suggested fields: name, contact, markets, languages, audience niche, portfolio links, relevant public engagement evidence, formats, availability, rates, rights restrictions, past delivery notes, and consent to be contacted for suitable opportunities.

Vetting: review recent content quality and consistency; audience relevance; suspicious engagement patterns; reliability; suitability for the brand; and rights/disclosure readiness. Do not imply a creator is approved simply because they completed a form. Record the review date and reviewer.

Brief template: business context, audience, offer, objective, message, proof points approved by the client, prohibited claims, required disclosure, format, duration, distribution channel, CTA, due dates, revisions, content rights, paid usage, and compensation.

Campaign process: client-approved brief -> creator shortlist -> cost/rights approval -> creator agreement -> draft -> one consolidated client review -> final asset -> posting or handover as agreed -> measurement -> closing report.

No automatic publishing to social accounts is implemented. Publishing requires the correct account, explicit approval, and a separately configured integration or manual owner.

## 7. Website and systems delivery

Site Rescue: reproduce reported issues, separate urgent blockers from improvements, estimate effort, agree a backup/rollback plan, implement in a branch, verify forms and phone journeys, and record acceptance.

Website Build: discovery, wireframe/content approval, regional implementation, accessible forms, metadata, redirect map, production checks, and handover. Offer a monthly care plan after acceptance, not as a hidden launch requirement.

Systems: discovery and data map before development. Agree roles, backups, migrations, exception handling, and ownership. Payment integrations require the customer's authorised provider account and production approval. Never use live money as an unannounced test.

Care plan: define included hours, response windows, supported systems, backup responsibility, excluded work, escalation path, and monthly reporting. Do not sell 24/7 support unless it is actually staffed.

## 8. Measurement

Track by region and acquisition source: website visits (only after analytics is configured), completed scans, enquiries, qualified enquiries, proposals, accepted projects, trial kickoffs, trial completions, paid conversions, monthly recurring service revenue, delivery hours, direct costs, contribution margin, and cancellations.

Important denominators: lead qualification rate = qualified / enquiries; trial conversion = paid retainers / completed trials; utilisation = billable delivery hours / available delivery hours. Keep the period and cohort consistent. No success targets are claimed as actual results.

For each campaign choose one useful business measure (qualified enquiries, appointments, or tracked orders) and supporting content measures. Explain attribution limits. Avoid reporting views as guaranteed revenue.

## 9. Ninety-day execution plan

Days 1-14: confirm legal/trading details, contract templates, creator availability, capacity, price floors, and launch owners. Configure domain/CDN, notifications/admin access, and speed diagnostics. Test the actual production customer journey before opening applications publicly.

Days 15-30: run a small first cohort, sell focused website repairs/builds, document delivery time and objections, and hold a weekly review. Use labelled demonstrations until real client publication permission exists.

Days 31-60: complete trials, propose monthly plans, collect consented feedback, tighten briefs and revision limits, and publish factual case studies with evidence. Reduce or pause trials that consume more capacity than planned.

Days 61-90: compare cohort economics and retention, improve the offer, and expand creator supply only where demand and fulfilment justify it. Test global acquisition with a small separate cohort before scaling spend.

## 11. Review rhythm

Daily: new enquiries, active approvals, delivery blockers. Weekly: pipeline, trial capacity, creator commitments, cash commitments, and overdue work. Monthly: customer retention, contribution by offer, system reliability, and what to stop selling or improve.

The business is not fully operational merely because the website exists. This rebuild supplies the public offer, enquiry capture, lead desk, scan/report flow, and operating model; human fulfilment, contracts, payments, creator supply, and account connections still need real owners.
